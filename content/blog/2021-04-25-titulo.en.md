A share is the smallest fraction of a corporation's capital. Shares can be common (with voting rights at shareholder meetings) or preferred (with priority in the distribution of profits).

Earnings per Share (EPS) = Net Income / Number of shares

### Gross Margin

Gross Margin is a metric used to identify a company's gross profitability. It's the ratio between Gross Profit and Net Revenue.

Gross Margin = (Gross Profit / Net Revenue) x 100

**Example:**
GM = 2,012.97 M / 9,284.30 M x 100 = 21.68% Gross Margin

The higher a company's gross margin, the more the company earns from selling its products.

More competition = margins tend to be tighter

Efficiency in generating profit

Possible distortion:

Temporary periods of high sales - it's important to analyze the history

TIM 53.69% / VIV 47% / OIBR 21.68%

To make it clearer, let's look at an example.

Say a company earns R$10 from selling a pen and producing it costs R$6. The gross profit, or gross margin, of each product would be **R$4**, and the gross margin percentage 40%. In other words, for each real sold, R$ 0.40 of profit was generated.

First, it tells you how much of a company's revenue is kept as profit. So if the gross margin is 22%, it means the company made R$0.22 of profit for each real of revenue.

### ROE

ROE (Return on Equity) is a profitability indicator used to determine how efficient a company is at generating profit from its resources.

ROE = Net Income / Shareholders' Equity (Assets - Liabilities)
OIBR3 = -10.528 B / 7.769 B * 100 = -135%

### Net Debt/EBITDA

EBITDA = Operating Profit = net revenue – cost of goods sold – operating expenses – net financial expenses.

Q1 2020
Recurring EBITDA = Operating Revenue - Operating Costs/Expenses

Recurring EBITDA = 4749 - 3216 = 1533

EBITDA = Recurring EBITDA + Non-recurring Items

EBITDA = 1533 + 367 = 1900

Q4 2020
Recurring EBITDA = Operating Revenue - Operating Costs/Expenses

Recurring EBITDA = 4777 - 3286 = 1491

EBITDA = Recurring EBITDA + Non-recurring Items

EBITDA = 1491 + 175 = 1666

The Net Debt/EBITDA ratio is used to analyze a company's level of indebtedness. Its result shows the number of years a company would take to pay off its net debt in a scenario where EBITDA stays constant.
