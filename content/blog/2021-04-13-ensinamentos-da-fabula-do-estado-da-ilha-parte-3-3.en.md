### 1. Opportunity cost and comparative advantage

How is it possible that, even with the rancher being better at both, the farmer's specialization increases production and benefits both?

Let's explain this puzzle with a few examples:

On the island there's a merchant - the best negotiator on the island - who can make a profit of 400 coins per hour buying and selling. There's another, less experienced merchant who makes a profit of 10 coins per hour - much less than the first one. So he decided to become a boat "mechanic" and charges 15 coins per hour to fix boats. A job usually takes him 2 hours - a total cost of 30 coins.

The best merchant on the island owns many boats, and when one has a problem he decides to fix it himself to "save" the 30 coins. He can do the same job in 1 hour, which means he's also better at fixing boats.

**Concept:** Absolute advantage - the comparison between producers of a given good based on their productivity.

**Concept:** Opportunity cost - what we give up to obtain something.

**Concept:** Comparative advantage - the comparison between producers of a good based on their opportunity costs.

Comparing opportunity costs, the best merchant is giving up 400 coins per hour to "save" the 30 coins he'd pay to have his boat fixed. Meanwhile, the other merchant gives up 10 to earn 15. In other words, the best merchant is better off focusing on his work as a merchant and paying for the service.

When someone specializes in something where they have a comparative advantage, the economy's total production increases.

Trade can benefit every member of society because it allows people to specialize in activities where they have a comparative advantage.

### 2. Why productivity is so important

A country's standard of living depends on its ability to produce goods and services. Let's think about an extremely simple society of just 1 person: their quality of life will depend on the amount of goods and services (collecting water, hunting animals, picking coconuts, etc.) they produce.

**Concept:** Productivity - the amount of goods and services a worker can produce per hour of work.

Back to the 1-person society: if they increase their productivity - by finding a better fishing spot, for example - they can spend more time resting or making their clothes, for example.

### 3. Saving enables capital accumulation in an economy

First, let's recall the factors of production:

* Land - natural resources
* Capital - a produced factor of production (fishing rod, ladder, etc.)
* Labor - human capital (education)

The larger a society's stock of capital, the more goods and services it can produce. In other words, to increase future productivity, current resources need to be invested in producing capital.

> **Concept: Trade-off**
>
> A conflict of choice: a decision where you have to give up one thing in exchange for another.

Saving, as we saw before, is allocating consumption over time: refraining from consuming in the present to consume better and more in the future.

Saving generates investment, which generates capital accumulation, which generates economic growth, and in the future everyone consumes more and better.
