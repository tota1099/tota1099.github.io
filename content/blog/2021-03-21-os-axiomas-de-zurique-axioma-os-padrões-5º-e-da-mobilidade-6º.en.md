#### THE 5th MAJOR AXIOM: ON PATTERNS

"Chaos is not dangerous until it begins to look orderly!"

There is no order in the financial market. Anyone who tries to find order and patterns in it is jumping to conclusions, because it has no patterns at all; it is absolute chaos. There is no formula or rule.

##### THE 5th MINOR AXIOM

Beware the historian's trap

Nothing guarantees that history will repeat itself. Sometimes it does, but it's impossible to predict when. Don't try to find patterns that repeated in the past hoping they will repeat again.

##### THE 6th MINOR AXIOM

Beware the chartist's illusion

This is an extension of the historian's trap, where one assumes that because of some historical repetition, something specific will happen to stocks. For example: in the past, every time the country's president changed, the stock plummeted. That's not a rule! Just because it happened in the past doesn't mean it will happen again.

##### THE 7th MINOR AXIOM

Beware the illusion of correlation

The human mind creates correlations and illusions of causality that don't exist. For example: we may see a correlation where every time stock A goes up, stock B goes down. That may have happened many times, but it's a false illusion of order. Be careful with the correlations your mind creates.

##### THE 8th MINOR AXIOM

Beware the gambler's fallacy

There are days when we think it's our "lucky day" and everything will go right. Be careful with that, because it can give you the confidence to take big risks and lose a lot of money!

#### THE 6th MAJOR AXIOM: ON MOBILITY

"Avoid putting down roots. They impede motion."

Don't fall in love with a specific company or stock. You need to be rational and know when to get in and out of the market. The goal is to make money; if you find a better opportunity, don't stay tied to your current company just because it gave you good returns in the past.

##### THE 9th MINOR AXIOM

Do not become trapped in a souring venture because of sentiments like loyalty or nostalgia

Our goal is to make money, and if we need to sell that stock we like so much because we speculate it will drop a lot soon, don't stay stuck out of loyalty or gratitude. For example: a couple who refuses to sell their house for an excellent price because their children grew up in it. A few months later the house loses value and they lose a lot of money.
