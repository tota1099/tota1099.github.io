A strong currency is one issued by a stable country that manages, over time, to maintain or even increase its value, even during global economic crises. A currency can also be considered strong because it is widely used in trade with other countries.

Strong currency - liquidity, currency parity


Examples of strong currencies:

USD - US Dollar

The currency issued by the United States is used as a reference all over the world. Besides the United States, the "US dollar" is also the official currency of other countries, such as Timor-Leste, Ecuador, El Salvador, Panama and Puerto Rico. It is also the "world's monetary reference currency", meaning it's accepted in almost every country in the world for exchange with the local currency.

EUR - Euro

The Euro is the official currency of the Eurozone, made up of Germany, Austria, Belgium, Cyprus, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Estonia, Finland, France, Greece, Ireland, Italy, Latvia, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, the Netherlands and Portugal. It's a widely used currency, considering how many world powers use it.


What are the advantages of a strong currency?

There are countless benefits for the country's economy. One of them is that the currency keeps its value high and, even in times of economic crisis - which can cause it to lose some value - it remains among the strongest on the market.
