#### THE 11th MAJOR AXIOM: ON STUBBORNNESS

"If it doesn't pay off the first time, forget it."

In the world of speculation, we have to be careful with persistence. For example, don't keep insisting on a company you lost money on, thinking it owes you something. If you have a better deal, get out and go to the other one.

##### THE 15th MINOR AXIOM

Never try to save a bad investment by "averaging down".

There's a technique called "averaging down" and it's a tempting trap. It works like this: if you buy a stock at 100.00 and it drops to 50, you just buy another share and your average price becomes 75 ((100 + 50) / 2). Excellent. Not really! This doesn't reduce your loss; it just creates an apparent illusion, which can lead to an even bigger loss. You still have the same loss.

- - -

#### THE 12th MAJOR AXIOM: ON PLANNING

"Long-range plans engender the dangerous belief that the future is under control. It is important never to take your own long-range plans, or other people's, seriously."

Planning is extremely important, but be careful! Just because you planned something doesn't mean it will happen. It's important not to take long-term plans too seriously. Taking them seriously can give the false impression that we control the future, and as we've seen before, that's impossible.

##### THE 16th MINOR AXIOM

Shun long-term investments.

You can't know what will happen tomorrow, but you have a good chance of getting it right, because there's rarely much change from one day to the next. Over 1 week, that becomes less likely. 1 month is unpredictable. With that in mind, why would you invest thinking 20 or 30 years ahead?

- - -

#### Conclusion

This book is essential for anyone just getting started in the world of investing. It gives very important tips for day-to-day decisions and shows that the investment world isn't a wonderland. It takes dedication, discipline and a lot of care with the traps that exist in the market. There are no huge easy gains.
