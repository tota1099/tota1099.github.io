I discovered the minimalist philosophy by watching the documentary [Minimalism](https://www.netflix.com/title/80114460). In short, it tells the story of 2 young men who had traditional jobs chasing professional success, discovered a new, lighter and happier way of living, and decided to leave everything behind to spread the philosophy around the world.

The subject really interested me, so I went looking for articles and books about it, and I even bought and read the book **The Joy of Less**, A Minimalist Guide to Declutter, Organize, and Simplify (Francine Jay), which gives lots of tips on how to bring the minimalist philosophy into your life.

## **Minimalism is having only your favorite things**

After learning about this philosophy, I talked to some people about it, and most of them think minimalism means living with the bare minimum, with a minimal number of clothes and no technology. In fact, minimalism is living with what's necessary and with what you like! As the book The Joy of Less puts it, people usually own 3 kinds of things:

### **Useful things**

Practical, functional items that help us get things done. Have you ever watched a video about survival gear? It's amazing to see how little we really need to survive. Shelter, clothes, water, food, and a few essential items like a pot to cook with. In short, everything you use often and that **adds value** to your life.

An important note: it's not enough for a thing to be useful, it has to be used! There's no point in having 50 pots and using only 1. The other 49 pots aren't useful things!

### **Beautiful things / things we like**

Items that aren't necessarily needed or functional, but that we like or find beautiful. Examples are paintings, a plate collection, and so on. But be careful with these items! If you have paintings you love but they're locked away in your basement, maybe you don't like them that much after all!

These items deserve a prominent spot in your home, since you like them so much. If an item stays hidden or unused, it falls into the category of unnecessary things.

### **Useless / duplicate / unnecessary things**

These are the items that don't fit the 2 previous categories. Usually it's something we're emotionally attached to and don't want to get rid of. You know those toys you had as a kid and kept until today? You probably keep them because they bring back good memories... but do you really need to keep all of them? Maybe it makes more sense to keep one specific toy that holds all those memories.

Another example is travel souvenirs: do you really need to buy something on every trip to "prove" you were there? Personally, I still have some items from trips I took that I don't use and that sit in storage, and I know how hard it is to let go of them, but it's necessary.

## Minimalist practices

To live a minimalist life and keep only the essentials, people usually follow some good practices. Below are some practices that **I** personally follow:

### Donating

Since I was little, my mother taught me to donate my clothes to people who need them more. Today, with minimalism, I keep the practice and have a fixed rule: whenever I get a piece of clothing, I donate the same number or more.

**Practice:** Whenever you get a piece of clothing, donate another one.

**Personal example:** I got 2 new t-shirts for Christmas. I donated 4 t-shirts :)

### **Save and make your dreams come true**

Do you think minimalism is only about physical things? We can be minimalist when spending money too, always trying to buy only what's essential or what truly fulfills us. With the extra money left over, plan to make a dream come true, like taking a trip or simply having lunch at a place you always wanted to try but could never afford.

**Practice:** Save 20% of your salary every month to make a dream come true every 6 months.

### Spam? Never heard of it

A simple example of minimalism is email spam. I know people who have been complaining about spam for years. Nowadays you can unsubscribe from ads or, if that option isn't available, block the address sending you spam.

**Practice:** Every time you get spam, try to unsubscribe from the mailing list or block the sender.

### Quality is the secret

I've seen people in the past celebrating that they bought 3 pairs of sneakers for R$ 100.00 (that person was me), and then complaining because the sneakers were uncomfortable and fell apart quickly. Today I've learned that cheap can end up being expensive, so I prefer to have very few sneakers, but comfortable, good-quality ones that I know will last for years.

**Practice:** Avoid buying things of questionable quality just because they're cheap. Pay a bit more and walk away with a good-quality product.

**Personal example:** I once bought 3 pairs of sneakers for R$ 100 on Netshoes and only wore 1 of them, because 2 were simply awful. Today I'd rather buy, for example, 1 pair of shoes for R$ 200 that's good quality and, above all, comfortable!

P.S.: Obviously, use common sense: cheap isn't always bad and expensive isn't always good!

## Conclusion

The idea of minimalism is to live a lighter life focused on quality of life.
