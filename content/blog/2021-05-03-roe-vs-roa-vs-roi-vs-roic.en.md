You've probably heard these acronyms: ROE, ROA, ROI and ROIC. But what does each one mean? What are they for? Where do they live? What do they eat? Jokes aside, the goal of this article is to explain each acronym with practical examples.

Choosing a company to invest in is usually not an easy task, but to feel more confident we study it a lot, analyzing its quarterly results, its history and important criteria. The acronyms above are indicators calculated from the company's results. Let's go through each one:

### ROE - Return on Equity

This fundamental indicator measures a company's ability to add value to itself using its own resources.

#### Formula

ROE = (Net Income / Shareholders' Equity) * 100

#### Example

CTEEP - CIA TRANSMISSÃO ENERGIA ELÉTRICA PAULISTA (TRPL4) in 2020:

Net Income: 3,559 Million
Shareholders' Equity: 13,307 Million

ROE = (3,559 Net Income / 13,307 Shareholders' Equity) * 100 = 26.74%

In practice, ROE is important when comparing two companies, "X" and "Y": if the first has a higher ROE than its competitor, you can say company "X" has more profit relative to its shareholders' equity. This comparison is fair because a company with 50 billion in equity, for example, will tend to have much more profit than one with 10 billion, but this analysis looks at profitability relative to equity, which makes the comparison fairer.

It can be masked by taking on debt

### ROA - Return on Assets

This fundamental indicator measures the profitability and total profit-generating capacity of a company's assets.

#### Formula

ROA = (Net Income / Total Assets) * 100

#### Example

CTEEP - CIA TRANSMISSÃO ENERGIA ELÉTRICA PAULISTA (TRPL4) in 2020:

Net Income: 3,559 Million
Total Assets: 26,505 Million

ROA = (3,559 Net Income / 26,505 Total Assets) * 100 = 13.42%

Comparing two companies, "X" and "Y", if the first has a higher ROE than its competitor, you can say company "X" has more profit relative to its shareholders' equity.

It can be masked by taking on debt

### ROIC - Return on Invested Capital

This fundamental indicator measures how much money a company can generate relative to the capital invested. This metric considers both the company's own capital and loans and third-party capital.

#### Formula

ROIC = EBIT / Book value

#### Example

CTEEP - CIA TRANSMISSÃO ENERGIA ELÉTRICA PAULISTA (TRPL4) in 2020:

NOPAT/EBIT: 4,427 Million

Book Value of Invested Capital: R$80 million

ROIC = (4,427 M / 2,858 M
) * 100 = 19.25%

Weighted average cost of capital - will the minimum return make it worthwhile?

### ROI - Return on Investment

### Formula

ROI = (Revenue - Cost) / Cost

Suppose you want to invest in a business and the initial investment is R$50,000.00. In one year, your investment returns R$100,000.00 to you, that is:

ROI = 100,000 – 50,000/50,000 x 100

ROI = 1 x 100: 100%

Drawback: it doesn't account for debt/taxes, it's very simplistic, and it's very limited for analysis.
